# Scope 3 Category 5 — Waste Generated in Operations

**Definition (Scope 3 Standard, ch. 5, table 5.4):** Emissions from
third-party disposal and treatment of waste generated in the reporting
company's owned or controlled operations in the reporting year. Covers solid
waste and wastewater. Only waste treatment by facilities **not owned or
controlled** by the reporter belongs here.

**Governing documents:** Corporate Value Chain (Scope 3) Standard (2011),
category 5 description; Technical Guidance for Calculating Scope 3 Emissions
(v1.0, 2013), category 5 chapter. Cross-cutting conventions (GWP set, EF
hierarchy, data quality, answering style) are defined in the `ghg-protocol`
skill and apply here without restatement.

## Boundary & classification

**In scope (minimum boundary):**
- Third-party treatment/disposal of operational solid waste: landfill,
  incineration (with or without energy recovery), recycling, composting,
  anaerobic digestion.
- Third-party treatment of wastewater generated in operations.
- The Guidance treats emissions from waste **transportation** to the treatment
  site as optional; many practitioners include it because published waste EFs
  (e.g., DEFRA) can be paired with a collection-transport add-on. State the
  choice either way.

**Optional additions:** emissions from treatment of wastewater at the point of
discharge beyond the treatment plant; future-year landfill emissions from
waste deposited in the reporting year are conventionally accounted using
factors that already integrate lifetime decay (both WARM and DEFRA landfill
factors are lifetime-integrated per tonne deposited — do not add a separate
"future emissions" term).

**Out of scope / routing:**
- Waste treated at facilities the reporter **owns or controls** (own landfill,
  own incinerator, on-site wastewater plant) → **scope 1** (and scope 2 for the
  facility's purchased energy).
- End-of-life treatment of **sold products** → category 12, not category 5.
- Waste generated by upstream suppliers → embedded in categories 1/2.
- Construction and demolition waste from the reporter's own projects: in
  category 5 if the reporter generates it in its operations; if a contractor
  generates and contracts disposal within a purchased construction service, it
  is embedded in category 1/2 — decide once, document, don't double count.

**The recycling allocation question.** Recycling sits at the boundary between
the generator's category 5 and the next user's category 1/purchased-goods
footprint. Two allocation conventions exist (Scope 3 Calculation Guidance,
category 5 + category 12 discussion of the recycled-content vs. closed-loop
methods):
- **Recycled-content method**: recycling burdens/benefits accrue to the user
  of the recycled material; the waste generator reports only **collection and
  processing up to the point of recycling** (sorting/MRF). This is why the
  DEFRA open-loop/closed-loop recycling factors are near-zero for most
  materials — they cover processing only.
- **Closed-loop method**: the generator may account for recycling process
  emissions and could, in an attributional sense, see avoided-virgin-material
  benefits — but **avoided emissions may never be netted from scope 3 totals**;
  they are reported, if at all, as a separate memo line.
The Guidance explicitly permits reporting only collection + transport to the
recycler; common practice is the recycled-content convention with near-zero
recycling factors. Disclose which convention you use.

**Energy-from-waste (WtE) credit:** if your waste is incinerated with energy
recovery, the displaced grid electricity is an **avoided emission** — report
it separately if desired, **never net it** against category 5 (Scope 3
Standard, ch. 9). DEFRA's "combustion" factor for waste already reflects this
convention: it counts the incineration CH4/N2O (and fossil CO2 for plastics)
without granting an energy credit inside the factor. Note that fossil CO2
from burning plastics is in-scope; biogenic CO2 from burning paper/food is
reported outside the scopes (see `ghg-protocol` §5) while the CH4/N2O stay in.

## Method ladder

Per the Scope 3 Calculation Guidance, category 5, three named methods, plus
practitioner extensions down the generic ladder:

| Tier | Method | Activity data needed | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Supplier-specific | Hauler/treatment-operator reported emissions allocated to your waste | Large sites with engaged waste vendors |
| 2 | Waste-type-specific | Tonnes by material × disposal route, × material/route EF (WARM, DEFRA) | Sites with waste audits or itemized hauler data |
| 3 | Average-data | Total tonnes × disposal route × mixed-waste EF | Sites with weighbridge/invoice tonnage only |
| 4 | Proxy | Per-FTE or per-ft² waste generation benchmark → tonnes → tier 3 | Small offices, missing sites |
| 5 | Spend-based | Waste-service spend ÷ average disposal cost → tonnes → tier 3; or waste spend × EEIO factor | Screening only |

### Method 1 — Supplier-specific

**When:** your hauler or treatment operator provides site- or
customer-allocated emissions data (increasingly available from national waste
majors).

```
CO2e = Σ (allocated emissions reported by waste treatment provider, per waste stream)
```

**Data required:** provider's methodology statement, allocation basis
(tonnage share), GWP set used, whether transport is included. **Pitfalls:**
providers often report using their own GWP set or include avoided-emission
credits — strip credits and re-normalize the GWP set to your inventory's.

### Method 2 — Waste-type-specific

**When to use:** you know tonnes by material type and disposal route. This is
the recommended tier for material category-5 footprints because landfill CH4
depends overwhelmingly on material: food and paper degrade anaerobically
(high CH4 per tonne — paper/board and food are the highest common streams),
while **plastics, metals, and glass do not degrade in landfill — their
landfill factor covers essentially transport/site operations only, near-zero
relative to organics**.

```
CO2e = Σ_materials Σ_routes  mass_m,r [tonnes] × EF_m,r [kgCO2e/tonne] / 1000
```

**EF sources:** EPA WARM (US; verify current version; MTCO2e/short ton,
includes avoided-emission elements you may need to strip for attributional
reporting — use WARM's landfill/combustion gross components carefully), UK
DESNZ/DEFRA GHG Conversion Factors "Waste disposal" tables (annual;
kgCO2e/tonne, attributional, transport-to-site included). See `ghg-protocol`
§7 for hierarchy.

**Method walk-through:** take tonnes by material and route from the hauler's
annual summary or a waste audit; for each material-route pair pull the
current-year DESNZ factor (or WARM gross component for US sites); multiply
and sum. Expect the landfilled mixed/organic tonnage to dominate the total
even when diverted tonnage is several times larger — route data matters more
than precision on diverted tonnage. **Pitfalls:** applying the mixed-waste
landfill factor to a plastics-only stream (overstates dramatically —
plastics-to-landfill is near-inert while mixed waste carries the
organic-fraction CH4); using WARM net factors (which embed avoided emissions)
as if attributional; short tons (WARM) vs. metric tonnes (DEFRA).

### Method 3 — Average-data

**When:** hauler invoices give total tonnes and route but not composition.

```
CO2e = Σ_routes  total mass_r [t] × mixed-waste EF_r [kgCO2e/t] / 1000
```

Use "commercial and industrial mixed" or "municipal mixed" factors matched to
your waste character. **Method walk-through:** split total tonnage by route
(landfill vs. WtE vs. recycling per the hauler's destination data); multiply
each route's tonnage by the current mixed-waste factor for that route. The
landfill share will dominate the result under current conventions
(WtE-combustion and recycling factors are low). **Pitfall:** hauler
"diversion rate" claims often count WtE as diversion; your inventory must
model the actual route.

### Method 4 — Proxy (per-FTE benchmark)

```
tonnes = FTE × waste-generation benchmark [t/FTE/yr]  → apply Method 3
```

Office benchmark: roughly **0.15–0.3 t/FTE/yr** of mixed office waste (a
waste-generation statistic, not an emission factor; varies by geography and
program maturity; derive your own from measured sites and apply to unmeasured
ones — a like-facility intensity per `ghg-protocol` §8).

### Method 5 — Spend-based

```
tonnes ≈ waste-service spend [$] ÷ average disposal cost [$/t]  → Method 3
   or
CO2e = waste-service spend [$] × EEIO waste-sector factor [kgCO2e/$]
```

US commercial mixed-waste disposal cost is on the order of **$60–110/t**
tipping plus hauling (a price statistic, highly regional — verify local).
Screening only; document the price assumption.

### Wastewater

Third-party (municipal) treatment of operational wastewater:

```
CO2e = volume [m³] × treatment EF [kgCO2e/m³] / 1000
```

Apply the current DESNZ "Water treatment" factor (UK average; covers
treatment CH4/N2O and plant energy — verify vintage and geographic fit). If
volume is unmetered, proxy from water supply volume × a discharge fraction
(often ~0.9–0.95 for offices). Industrial high-COD effluent needs IPCC 2006
GL vol. 5 ch. 6 COD-based CH4 estimation instead — flag and escalate.

**Relative-magnitude structure (methodology, retained without values):**
paper/board and food to landfill carry the highest per-tonne factors
(anaerobic CH4); mixed municipal/commercial landfill sits below the pure
organic streams; plastics, metals, and glass to landfill are near-inert;
recycling (processing-only convention), WtE combustion (no energy credit),
composting, and AD are all low — one to two orders of magnitude below
organic-to-landfill.

This skill intentionally quotes no factor values. When a quantitative answer
is needed, pull the current-year value from the named source.

## Unit and conversion traps

- **Short tons vs. metric tonnes**: WARM uses short tons; DEFRA uses tonnes.
  Mixing understates/overstates by ~10%.
- **Wet vs. dry tonnes**: hauler weighbridge data is wet mass; factors are
  built for wet as-collected waste. Sludge quoted in dry solids must be
  converted before applying a wet-basis factor.
- **Volume-billed waste** (dumpster lifts, cubic yards): convert with a
  density assumption (loose mixed office waste ~60–150 kg/m³; compacted
  ~250–500 kg/m³). Document the density; it is often the largest uncertainty.
- **Lifetime-integrated landfill factors**: do not additionally model decay
  years — the per-tonne factor already integrates the decay curve.
- **Avoided emissions embedded in "net" factors** (WARM net, some LCA
  recycling factors): strip to gross for scope 3 totals.
- **Biogenic CO2** from composting/AD/incineration of organics is outside the
  scopes; the published DEFRA factors already exclude it — don't add it back.

## Data collection & gap-filling

- **Hauler invoices / weighbridge tickets**: primary source for tonnage and
  route; request an annual customer summary by material and destination.
- **Waste audits / bin composition studies**: unlock Method 2; a one-week
  audit extrapolated annually is acceptable if seasonality is addressed.
- **Vendor portals** (national waste majors) often export tonnes by stream
  and sometimes vendor-computed CO2e — reconcile methodology before adopting.
- **Wastewater**: municipal sewer bills (volume or water-supply-based),
  discharge permits.
- **Gap-filling**: per-FTE or per-ft² intensity from measured sibling sites;
  annualize partial-year data pro-rata; flag filled values (`ghg-protocol` §8).
- Reconcile diversion-rate claims against invoiced tonnage before reporting.

## QA checks

- **Per-FTE sanity**: office category-5 totals typically land at
  ~0.02–0.15 tCO2e/FTE/yr; > 0.5 for an office suggests a unit error or a
  mislabeled route.
- **Landfill dominance check**: if recycling tonnage drives your CO2e total,
  a wrong (life-cycle) recycling factor is probably in use.
- **Diversion-rate sanity**: computed diversion (non-landfill ÷ total) should
  match hauler claims within a few points; investigate gaps.
- **Density audit** on any volume-to-mass conversions.
- **Year-over-year factor vintage**: DEFRA landfill factors move as capture
  assumptions update; disclose vintage changes (not a base-year trigger unless
  significant per `ghg-protocol` §6).

## Worked FAQ

**Q1. We send 40 t of office paper to recycling and 10 t to landfill. Which
dominates?** The landfilled tonnes, heavily. Paper to landfill carries one of
the highest per-tonne factors (anaerobic CH4), while paper to recycling gets
the near-zero processing-only factor under the recycled-content convention —
so a small landfilled stream outweighs a much larger recycled one. Pull the
current DESNZ factors to quantify.

**Q2. Our WtE provider says our waste generated electricity — can we subtract
the avoided grid emissions?** No. Report category 5 using the combustion
factor (which grants no energy credit); the avoided grid emissions (recovered
MWh × grid factor) may be disclosed as a separate avoided-emissions memo line
only, never netted (Scope 3 Standard, ch. 9).

**Q3. Spend-based screening from waste-service spend — how?** Divide spend by
a documented local disposal+haul cost ($/t) to estimate tonnes; split by an
assumed landfill/diverted route mix; apply current mixed-waste route factors
(Method 3). Screening grade only (tier 5) — flag for hauler-data upgrade if
material, and document both the price and route-mix assumptions.

**Q4. Our site discharges to municipal sewer with no effluent meter.** Use
metered water supply × a documented discharge fraction (~0.9 for offices) as
the volume, then multiply by the current DESNZ water-treatment factor.
Industrial high-COD effluent instead needs IPCC COD-based CH4 estimation.

**Q5. We operate our own on-site wastewater treatment plant — category 5?**
No. Direct CH4/N2O from your own plant → **scope 1**; its electricity →
scope 2. Only the residual sludge sent to a third party stays in category 5.

**Q6. WARM shows a negative net factor for recycling cardboard. Can I book a
negative?** No. That is a net life-cycle factor including avoided virgin
production. For your attributional inventory use collection/processing
emissions only (recycled-content convention, near zero) and, optionally,
report WARM-style net benefits separately as avoided emissions.
